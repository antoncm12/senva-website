Your plan for the next six months

Three things to focus on, and how to do them

Your plan follows fixed rules: each part is triggered by your results, your questionnaire or your wearable data, and each follows public health advice. It’s lifestyle advice made specific for you, not treatment.

Training

Eight weeks of strength and intervals

Start next week. Each week has two strength sessions, one interval session and two longer walks or rides. Together with your daily walking, this meets the WHO advice of 150–300 minutes of moderate activity a week and strength training on two days [18].

Your heart-rate zones

From your resting heart rate (60, from your ring) and an estimated maximum of 177 for your age (208 − 0.7 × age [25]), using the heart-rate reserve method [26]. If you have measured your maximum in a test, add it in the app and we’ll use it instead.

Strength A · Monday

About 45 minutes, with dumbbells and a bench

Strength B · Friday

About 45 minutes, with dumbbells, a bench and a band or cable

How hard, and how to progress

  • Warm up for 5–8 minutes, then do one light set of the first exercise.
  • Choose a weight you could lift two or three more times at the end of each set.
  • Weeks 1–2: two sets of each exercise, to learn the movements. Weeks 3–8: three sets.
  • When you can do the top of the range in every set with good form, add the smallest step of weight (1–2 kg).
  • Rest 60–90 seconds between sets.

4 × 4 intervals · Wednesday

  • 10 minutes easy, in your moderate zone (107–129).
  • 4 minutes hard at 150–168, then 3 minutes easy. Repeat four times.
  • 5 minutes easy to finish.
  • Weeks 1–2: three rounds of 3 minutes. From week 3: four rounds of 4 minutes.

This is the 4 × 4 method from Norwegian research [27]. Running, cycling, rowing, uphill walking or a cross-trainer all work. Hard means you can only say a few words.

Before you start. If you have a heart condition, high blood pressure or another long-term condition, if you have had chest pain or dizziness, or if you haven’t trained hard for a long time, talk to a doctor before you do the intervals. Stop and seek care if you feel chest pain, faintness or unusual breathlessness.

Why you see this: your ring shows 165 active minutes a week, up from 70, so you now meet the WHO advice for moderate activity. The next step is strength and some harder training, which build the fitness that research links to the pattern behind your heart and circulation indicator.

Food

Your food targets

Your amounts come from your questionnaire. The targets come from the Nordic Nutrition Recommendations 2023 [20]; the green band is the target.

Also eat beans, lentils and peas often, use rapeseed or olive oil, and choose low-fat milk products in moderate amounts. Keep processed meat, salty snacks and sugary drinks to a minimum.

Protein: 100–130 g a day

While you build strength, aim for about 1.2–1.6 g of protein per kilo of body weight a day (you: 82 kg) [28]. Spread it over three or four meals of 25–40 g.

Good sources: fish, eggs, quark and yoghurt, beans and lentils, chicken, tofu.

Simple swaps for you

  • Swap two red-meat dinners a week for fish: salmon, herring or mackerel.
  • Add a handful of unsalted nuts to breakfast or as a snack.
  • Add a portion of vegetables to lunch and dinner, and fruit or berries to breakfast.

A day that hits your targets

About 105 g of protein, 700 g of vegetables, fruit and berries, and well over 90 g of whole grains.

Why you see this: one inflammation-linked site (SOCS3) and one blood-fat-linked site (ABCG1) are slightly less favourable, and you eat fish about once a week.

Supplements

Your next box

Chosen from your results and questionnaire. Food comes first: supplements add to a good diet, they don’t replace it. Check with a doctor or pharmacist if you take medicines, are pregnant or have a long-term condition.

Not in your box, and why

  • NMN, NR, resveratrol and similar “anti-ageing” supplements: the evidence in people is too weak.
  • Metformin and rapamycin: these are prescription medicines, not supplements.
  • High-dose vitamins and minerals: more is not better, and some are harmful in high doses.

Alcohol and sleep

Alcohol: from about 11 to about 6 drinks a week

You now drink on about four days a week. Your next step: at least five alcohol-free days a week, and fewer drinks on the days you drink. Less is better for health; none is best [19, 20].

  • Choose your alcohol-free days in advance, including one weekend day.
  • Alternate each drink with water, and try alcohol-free versions of what you like.
  • Keep alcohol well before bedtime: it makes sleep lighter.

Sleep: 7 h 10 min a night

Up from 6 h 40 min. Adults need 7 hours or more [29]. Your bedtime varies by about 70 minutes from night to night; try to keep it within 30 minutes, also at weekends.

  • Aim for lights out by 23:00 on weekdays and weekends.
  • Get daylight in the morning and keep the bedroom cool and dark.

What your ring shows after a drink

On the 9 nights in the last month when you tagged a drink, your night-time heart rate was 4 beats per minute higher, and your heart-rate variability 7 ms lower, than on other nights. Many people see this: alcohol makes the heart work harder during sleep.

Why you see this: your alcohol-linked pattern is slightly raised, your liver and metabolism indicator is above your calendar age, and you tagged drinks in your ring app.

About this report

What it is, and what it isn’t

A well-being service

Senva Health estimates your epigenetic age and pace of ageing, shows lifestyle-linked methylation patterns, and helps you act on them with a plan and a care team. It does not diagnose, predict or treat any disease.

Epigenetic, not genetic

We measure methylation, chemical marks that change during life. We don’t read your DNA sequence, so the report says nothing about inherited conditions.

Your data

Your sample is analysed in Europe and your data is handled under the GDPR. You choose what is shared and with whom, and you can download or delete your results at any time.
